## Short-answer and case items

### QB-U6-08 [L27 · CLO6 · Evaluate · A · case] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-92.** A new sponsor arrives with a mandate to "reset
expectations" — code for a re-baseline before any assessment. State what you
offer her, what you refuse, and why the refusal serves her stated goal.

**Marking criteria:** offer = a verified position within two weeks (decision
pack with options) (2); refuse = no commitments or re-baseline before
assessment (2); the service argument — a truthful baseline makes her later
wins real; a cosmetic one makes her third quarter impossible (2).

### QB-U6-09 [L28 · CLO6 · Evaluate · A · case] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-99.** The whole team pads estimates 25–40% because the
baseline gets weaponized. Classify the problem and design the intervention.

**Marking criteria:** classification — systemic response to incentive
design, not individual dishonesty (2); intervention — estimates carry
ranges with confidence, commitments at a chosen percentile, calibration
reviews blame-free (2); what fails — zero tolerance on padding (produces
better liars) (2).

### QB-U6-10 [L28 · CLO6 · Evaluate · A · case] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-100.** A research group requests patient data outside
consent scope; their funding expires in two weeks. Rule on the request.

**Marking criteria:** consent analysis — purpose differs from scope;
de-identification changes regulatory exposure, not the ethical gap (2);
governance path — board's own cycle; the funding deadline gets zero
decisional weight and is named as such (2); alternatives offered with
energy — consented cohort, synthetic data, aggregate statistics (2).

### QB-U6-12 [L30 · CLO6 · Evaluate · A · case] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-94.** A 9-year-old ledger still runs read-only; every
closure attempt dies when a department says "we might need it." Design the
decommission decision that ends the cycle.

**Marking criteria:** separate data (retained per regulation, exported with

(2); a cutover decision memo with a date (2).

### QB-U6-13 [L28 · CLO6 · Apply · I · short] (4 marks)
A project's AI-use disclosure should record which three things?

**Answer:** where AI assistance was used (artifacts/decisions), what was
accepted vs rejected from its output, and the human accountability line —
who verified and owns the result. 1⅓ each (round to 4).

### QB-U6-14 [L30 · CLO6 · Apply · I · short] (4 marks)
Name three artifacts a closure checklist must confirm and one "political"
closure act.

**Answer:** signed acceptance, retention/archival verification, lessons
register with conditions+mechanisms, benefits/operations owner handover —
any three (1 each); political act e.g. decommission memo with a date,
recognition of the team, sponsor-facing benefits statement (1).

### QB-U6-15 [L29 · CLO6 · Analyze · I · short] (4 marks)
Why can a project that met all its constraints still fail its benefits
review — and which artifact, built at initiation, is the defense?

**Answer:** constraints measure delivery, not outcome; benefits depend on
adoption/deployment and model-world lift that the project controls only
partially. Defense: a benefits case with an owner, measurable baselines,
and a review cadence agreed at initiation (CS-104/105 logic). 2 + 2.

### QB-U6-16 [L27 · CLO6 · Apply · I · short] (4 marks)
State the constraint resolution order CS-88 teaches when scope, budget, and
date are all under pressure with a contractual date, and the one yield that
is forbidden.

**Answer:** scope yields first (defer non-mandated scope), budget second
(one deliberate re-baseline), date holds last; quality floor is the
forbidden yield — defect escape at a contractual go-live is never the
savings source. 1 per ordering element + 1 for the quality floor.

### QB-U6-17 [L28 · CLO6 · Evaluate · A · case] (5 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-90.** Map the three obligations in play when a falsified
compliance result is discovered — professional, regulatory, employment —
and state what "proportionality" applies to.

**Marking guide:** professional — honesty/responsibility values compel
correction (1.5); regulatory — the false submission is the organization's
exposure regardless of intent (1.5); employment — pressure mitigates for
the person, never for the act: proportionality attaches to the sanction on
the person, not to the duty to correct (2).

### QB-U6-18 [L30 · CLO6 · Remember · F · short] (3 marks)
Define "administrative closure" vs "contract closure" in one line each.

**Answer:** administrative — internal verification, lessons, release of
resources, archival; contract — confirming deliverables and formal
acceptance with each vendor, closing open claims. 1.5 each.
