## MCQ items

### QB-U2-01 [L05 · CLO1 · Remember · F · MCQ] (1 mark)
A predictive (waterfall) life cycle is most appropriate when:
A. Requirements are volatile and learning is high
B. Requirements are stable and the scope is well understood
C. The team is distributed
D. Compliance is involved

**Answer: B.** (D alone does not force predictive — regulated agile exists.)

### QB-U2-02 [L05 · CLO1 · Understand · F · MCQ] (1 mark)
The defining feature of an incremental life cycle is:
A. Iterations refine the same deliverable repeatedly
B. The product is produced in successive usable additions
C. Scope is discovered continuously
D. Nothing ships until the end

**Answer: B.** (A describes iterative; D is predictive/serial.)

### QB-U2-03 [L06 · CLO1 · Understand · F · MCQ] (1 mark)
A project charter's authority comes from:
A. The project team's consensus
B. The sponsor/performing organization formally authorizing the PM
C. The PM's technical credibility
D. The CCB's first meeting

**Answer: B.**

### QB-U2-04 [L06 · CLO1 · Apply · I · MCQ] (1 mark)
Which belongs in a charter's success criteria rather than its objectives?
A. "Improve student services"
B. "Cut service-request turnaround from 6 days to 2 by semester start, verified by ticket data"
C. "Deliver a world-class portal"
D. "Use modern technology"

**Answer: B.** (Measurable, dated, verifiable = criterion.)

### QB-U2-05 [L07 · CLO5 · Understand · F · MCQ] (1 mark)
A stakeholder with high power and low interest should be managed by:
A. Monitoring with minimal effort
B. Keeping satisfied
C. Close management
D. Ignoring until they complain

**Answer: B.** (Keep satisfied; high-power/high-interest = manage closely.)

### QB-U2-06 [L08 · CLO5 · Apply · I · MCQ] (1 mark)
Tailoring a heavyweight template down to a 6-week project is justified when:
A. The template is organizationally mandated
B. Each removed artifact's function is covered by a lighter mechanism
C. The PM dislikes paperwork
D. The budget is small

**Answer: B.** (Tailoring = replace, not just remove — the function must
survive.)

### QB-U2-07 [L05 · CLO1 · Apply · I · MCQ] (1 mark)
A hybrid life cycle is most defensible when:
A. Nobody can agree on requirements or compliance
B. A fixed regulatory/commercial spine frames iterative delivery inside it
C. The team wants "some agile and some waterfall"
D. The sponsor is indifferent

**Answer: B.** (CS-07's regulated-data-product pattern; C is the anti-pattern.)

## Short-answer items

### QB-U2-08 [L05 · CLO1 · Analyze · I · short] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-06.** Five vignettes: (1) server replacement, (2) churn
model with evolving features, (3) ERP with fixed scope, (4) portal built in
usable quarterly releases, (5) research spike with unknown feasibility.
Recommend a life cycle for each with a one-line reason.

**Answer:** 1 predictive (stable, known); 2 iterative/adaptive (learning
loop); 3 predictive (fixed scope, contractual); 4 incremental (usable
additions); 5 adaptive/spike (highest uncertainty). 1 mark per match + 1 for
reasoning quality.

### QB-U2-09 [L06 · CLO1 · Apply · I · short] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-08.** Convert this vague brief into two charter elements:
"The portal should be better and delivered soon; the VC wants it visible."
Produce one SMART objective and one success criterion.

**Marking guide:** objective = specific, measurable, achievable, relevant,
time-bound transformation of "better/soon" (3); criterion = verification
method + threshold + date (2); explicit sponsor/VC role stated (1).

### QB-U2-10 [L07 · CLO5 · Apply · I · short] (5 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-09.** List four stakeholders of a hospital analytics
project and, for each, one exchange (what they need from the project / what
the project needs from them).

**Marking guide:** 1 per named stakeholder with plausible exchange;
exchanges must be two-directional for full marks (e.g., clinicians provide
workflow constraints and receive usable alerts). Stakeholders that only
"receive reports" score 0.5 each.

### QB-U2-11 [L07 · CLO5 · Analyze · A · case] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-10.** A registrar holds high power and low interest until
semester start, when interest spikes and their unstaffed requests become
urgent. Design the engagement strategy across the project timeline.

**Marking criteria:** keep-satisfied cadence during low-interest phase
(monthly one-pager) (2); planned interest ramp before semester start with
named engagement date (2); escalation path for the urgent phase — pre-agreed
decision channel, not first-come panic (2).

### QB-U2-12 [L08 · CLO5 · Evaluate · A · case] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-11.** A 6-week project is handed a 40-page heavyweight
template suite. Propose the three tailoring cuts you would make first and
the lighter mechanism that preserves each artifact's function.

**Marking guide:** e.g., full risk-management plan → one-page register with
weekly review (function: risk visibility); formal comms plan → stakeholder
grid + standing agenda (function: information flow); full QA plan → DoD +
review checklist (function: quality floor). 1 mark per cut + 1 per preserved
function; cuts without replacement mechanisms score half.

### QB-U2-13 [L06 · CLO1 · Understand · F · short] (3 marks)
State what a charter gives the project manager that no other artifact does.

**Answer:** formal authority to apply organizational resources to the
project; accept "the sponsor's authorization of the PM and the project's
existence." 3 marks for the authority idea with the sponsor source.

### QB-U2-14 [L05 · CLO1 · Evaluate · A · case] (5 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-07.** A regulated data product needs a fixed privacy
review gate but iterative model development. Defend a hybrid design: which
elements are predictive, which adaptive, and why the gate cannot be
iterated away.

**Marking criteria:** predictive spine = consent/privacy gate + release
windows (2); adaptive interior = model/feature development (2); gate is
non-iterable because legal exposure attaches to deployment, not drafts —
each deployment crosses the gate (2, cap 6).

### QB-U2-15 [L07 · CLO5 · Analyze · I · short] (4 marks)
Why does the power/interest grid place "keep satisfied" stakeholders above
"monitor" even when both are currently quiet?

**Answer:** power converts into project-ending decisions at any time;
interest can spike on external events (deadlines, press, elections) — the
grid position is a current snapshot, and the strategy is about the cost of
being wrong. 2 + 2.

### QB-U2-16 [L08 · CLO5 · Apply · I · short] (4 marks)
Give two project attributes that push tailoring *up* (more ceremony) and
two that push it *down*.

**Answer:** up — regulated/safety-critical context, many external
stakeholders, contractual penalties, geographically distributed teams
(any two); down — short duration, small team, internal-only stakeholders,
low consequence of failure (any two). 1 per correct attribute.

### QB-U2-17 [L06 · CLO1 · Analyze · A · case] (6 marks)
**Case anchor: CS-08.** A sponsor says "don't bother with a charter, just
start — we all know what we want." Write the three-sentence response that
secures the charter without confrontation.

**Marking criteria:** names what the charter protects the *sponsor* with
(a defensible funding decision, a success test) (3); proposes a light
one-page version with a 48-hour turnaround (2); no concession on authority
— the PM cannot commit resources without it (1).

### QB-U2-18 [L05 · CLO1 · Remember · F · short] (3 marks)
Name the four life-cycle types taught in L05 and one distinguishing signal
for each.

**Answer:** predictive (stable scope), iterative (refine toward a fixed
target), incremental (successive usable additions), adaptive (scope
discovered through iterations). ¾ mark each, round up.
