# Answer key — CS-76 · First thirty days on a failing program

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Evidence-first takeover

## Reasoning

Week 1: listen — one-on-ones with every team lead, read the last three monthly reports against raw data, sit in on two standups without speaking. The single deliverable: a written statement of what you know, what you don't, and what you'll know by when. Week 2: verify the EVM data's integrity (CS-51's lesson — committed costs and the verbal change order), walk the critical path with the people who own it, meet the vendor's executive sponsor. Week 3: draft the re-forecast posture with options, not a number. Week 4: present the day-21 framework — commit to EAC₁-based honesty with a scope-preservation option and a descope option, each with evidence requirements. Quick wins: publish the commitment ledger weekly (control made visible); kill the two zombie workstreams everyone privately knows are dead (decisiveness without new promises). What you will NOT do: re-baseline in week 1, promise a date, change the org structure, or relitigate the past PM's decisions publicly. 'Something I can believe' = a forecast with stated error bars and a track record of kept micro-commitments, not optimism.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Re-baseline immediately: wipes the credibility deficit onto the baseline — sometimes right, never in week 1, because the assessment that justifies it does not exist yet.
## Facilitation

Grade the not-do list hardest; incoming leaders are graded on what they resist doing in month one.
