# Answer key — CS-92 · Sponsor reset: the new vice-chancellor

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Assessment-first transition

## Reasoning

The offer: 'Within two weeks you will have a verified position — cost, schedule, risk, and the three decisions you actually own. I ask one thing first: no commitments to your stakeholders from me before day 14, so your first number is one you can defend.' This gives her what the arrival mandate denies her: a defensible position. The refusal: a re-baseline before assessment is not a reset, it is a fiction with a new date — propose instead the day-14 decision pack where a re-baseline, if the evidence supports one, is one of three options with their costs. Commitment review: each inherited commitment is classified — stands (the regulatory dates: facts, not favors), renegotiates (the go-live date's buffer, if the assessment supports it), or retires (promises made to the departing sponsor with no written basis). The political insight: her mandate says 'reset expectations', and the way to serve it is a truthful baseline that makes her later wins real — a cosmetic re-baseline makes her first quarter look good and her third quarter impossible. What she needs in week 1 that she won't ask for: the list of landmines — which commitments detonate when, so nothing surprises her.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Comply with the early re-baseline: short-term safety, long-term complicity — when the true position surfaces (it always does), the fiction carries both signatures.
## Facilitation

Ask who else in the organization knows the true CPI — the answer (the PMO, the finance partner, the team) is why cosmetic re-baselines always surface.
