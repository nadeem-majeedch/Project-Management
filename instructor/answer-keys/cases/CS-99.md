# Answer key — CS-99 · The padding dilemma

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** System-fix over moral fix

## Reasoning

Classification: this is a system failure wearing an honesty costume. Padding at 25–40% that lands at 85% success is not lying — it is the team restoring the buffer that the planning process removed; the baseline gets weaponized (variance is punished as failure rather than read as information), so rational agents re-add the safety the process stripped. The intervention removes the incentive: (1) separate the estimate conversation from the commitment conversation — estimates carry ranges with stated confidence, commitments are made at a chosen percentile (CS-65's logic); (2) estimate calibration reviews — past estimates vs actuals, team-level, blame-free (CS-14's correction factor institutionalized); (3) variance read as information: the question after a miss is 'what did we learn about our estimating', never 'who is responsible'. The 85% figure proves the baseline is achievable; it hides that the organization is paying 30% more for its projects than its honest forecasts would cost — that is the disclosure the sponsor needs, eventually: 'our baselines carry an embedded 30% buffer; here is the calibration data; here is what honest baselines would buy you (earlier starts, more projects) and cost you (a 60% hit rate until calibration improves).' Disclosure is staged because dropping the buffer overnight converts a systemic fix into a team punishment — the sponsor hears it when the calibration mechanism exists to receive it.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Zero tolerance on padding: treats a system output as a character flaw, produces better liars — the one intervention this case exists to rule out.
## Facilitation

Ask who benefits from the padded baseline — the team avoids pain, and the sponsor gets a success story; padding persists because both parties are secretly satisfied. That is why the fix must be structural.
