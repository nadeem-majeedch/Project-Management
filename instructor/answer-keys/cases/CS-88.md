# Answer key — CS-88 · Capstone feeder: the three-constraint recovery

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Ordered constraint resolution

## Reasoning

Resolution order: scope yields first (the descoped items from the original build — non-mandate features go to a post-go-live phase), quality floor holds (the one yield that is forbidden is defect escape at a contractual go-live), budget yields second (the re-baseline happens once, with the descope's savings and the mandate's cost in the same motion), date holds last (the contractual date is the one constraint that cannot yield without penalty — so everything else arranges around it). Trade-off table candidates: (A) full descope + re-baseline (date safe, budget +15%, scope deferred); (B) crash with added resources (date safe, budget +25%, quality risk from new-hands effect); (C) phased go-live (date technically held, scope split, stakeholder trust risk). Selection: A, with B's crash limited to the mandate's critical path only. Re-forecast: original EAC 25% over; descope recovers ~12 points; mandate adds ~10; net re-baseline ≈ +23% with stated efficiency assumptions (CS-82's evidence test applies). Stakeholder sequence: sponsor (the trade-off, first), CCB (the re-baseline), delivery team (the truth about their month-9 position), regulator (the mandate's new date, early), end users (post-go-live phase plan, last — expectations, not secrets). 'A bit of everything' fails because it spends the re-baseline credibility on none of the constraints fully.
