# Answer key — CS-81 · The board briefing after the leak

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Disclosure-grade briefing

## Reasoning

Structure: facts and timeline (62 hours, detection-to-fix); affected population and notification status; containment and verification evidence; regulatory and legal posture (counsel's section, minimal prose); unknowns register; accountability and next steps. The unknowns register is the credibility core: 'root cause of the misconfiguration chain — resolves day 5'; 'whether data was accessed, not just exposed — forensic timeline day 12'; 'regulatory notification threshold determination — counsel, day 7'. Written for the headline test: every sentence survives being read alone. Accountability: 'The exposure resulted from a change process that allowed production configuration without peer review; the owner of that control was my organization, and the accountability is mine' — one sentence, no passive constructions, no committee diffusion. VC's 'clean' and counsel's 'minimal' reconcile: clean means complete and plain, not short; counsel's minimalism applies to speculation, not to facts. Follow-up: weekly one-pager against the unknowns register until every line is dated-closed.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- None recorded.
## Facilitation

Read the accountability paragraph aloud twice — once fast, once slowly; the second reading is the board's. Language that survives slowness is disclosure-grade.
