# Answer key — CS-104 · Benefits shortfall at the six-month review

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Multiplicative benefits decomposition

## Reasoning

Lift ratio = 0.8/1.4 = 0.5714. Realized = 4,200,000 × 0.7 × 0.5714 = 1,680,000. Gap = 2,520,000. Decomposition: deployment component — 30% of the base unserved, worth 4,200,000 × 0.3 × 0.5714 ≈ 719,900 of the gap; lift component — the model's realized lift is 57% of promise across the covered base, worth the remainder ≈ 1,800,100 (verify: deployment-only shortfall assumes full lift on covered base: 4.2M × 0.7 = 2.94M vs full promise 4.2M → 1.26M; then lift shortfall on covered base: 2.94M × (1 − 0.5714) = 1.26M... recomputing cleanly: gap = 4.2M − 1.68M = 2.52M; deployment share = 4.2M×(1−0.7)×0.5714 = 719,892; lift share = 4.2M×0.7×(1−0.5714) = 1,260,108; interaction = 4.2M×0.3×0.4286 = 540,036 — the three parts sum to 2.52M ✓). Ownership: deployment (719.9k) — delivery lead; mechanism: the rollout plan for the remaining 30%, which is usually integration debt, not marketing; lift (1.26M) — model owner; mechanism: segment analysis (CS-74's slices) to find where lift is under-realizing, retrain or threshold work; interaction (540k) — joint, and it is why partial fixes mislead: closing deployment alone recovers only the deployment share plus the interaction's redeployment. Month-9 review: evidence — deployment percentage, per-segment realized lift, retention delta against baseline; decisions — continue/redirect the rollout, fund a model iteration, or re-baseline the benefit statement to what the evidence supports. The distinction that matters: a benefits shortfall is a PROJECT failure only if deployment is the cause; a lift shortfall is a modeling question, and conflating them produces both bad engineering and bad governance.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Re-baselining the benefit immediately to 1.68M: honest about reality, and it surrenders the 2.52M question before the two owned mechanisms have run — the month-9 review is where re-baselining belongs.
