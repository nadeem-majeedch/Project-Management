# Answer key — CS-78 · The delay claim defense

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Decompose-concede-counter

## Reasoning

Decomposition: 3 weeks client access delays — documented, concede with mitigation credit (vendor could have escalated earlier); 2 weeks shared causation — negotiate split; 2 weeks vendor schema redesign — counter-claim: the redesign was a vendor internal decision, evidenced by the design review minutes the vendor never updated into the schedule; the schedule baseline shows their float absorption, then none. Evidence map: access-request tickets with timestamps, the access grant emails, design-review minutes, the schedule updates (or their absence), the weekly reports where the redesign's impact should have appeared. Counter-position: concede 3, split 2, counter 2 — net exposure ≈ 4 weeks against 7 claimed. The strategic logic of conceding documented delays first: a defense that contests everything reads as evasion and invites the arbitrator to discount the legitimate counter. Records fix: weekly report includes a 'delay events' section with cause attribution agreed at the weekly meeting — attribution recorded contemporaneously is the entire ballgame.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Full contest: the 2 disputed weeks have genuinely mixed causation; contesting them invites deep discovery that will also expose your own records gaps.
## Facilitation

Ask what the vendor's claim would look like if YOUR weekly reports had captured the schema redesign — claims are won by the record you kept, not the lawyer you hire.
