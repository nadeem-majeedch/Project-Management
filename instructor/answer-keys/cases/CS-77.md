# Answer key — CS-77 · Merging two teams without losing either

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Agency-preserving merger

## Reasoning

Weeks: 1 — separate listening sessions, then a joint session that names the differences as data ('research optimizes for exploration, ops for reliability — the merged team needs both and that tension is the job'); 2 — joint working-agreement workshop where both sides' norms compete openly and the team selects; 3 — new ceremony calendar, definition of done, and the first merged sprint planned together. Lead transition: the non-selected lead gets a named, real role — architecture owner or model-reliability owner — with explicit decision rights (CS-66's matrix), announced in the same breath as the selection. Retention risks: the two transfer requesters get one-on-ones this week (their question is 'do I have a future here', answered concretely); the notebook-culture members fear process suffocation — the working agreement's review cadence is the answer. 'Best person' framing fails because it converts a management decision into a verdict on people; the design must give the losing side agency and a real job, not reassurance.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- External lead: removes the zero-sum optics, costs context and signals neither team was trusted — defensible if both internal candidates are genuinely unready.
## Facilitation

Ask who decides the working agreement — the manager or the team? The answer determines whether the agreement survives the first conflict.
