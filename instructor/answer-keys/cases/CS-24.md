# Answer key — CS-24 · Contract-type selection scenarios

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Incentive mechanics + type matching

## Reasoning

Target price = 880,000. POTA = 800,000 + (950,000 − 880,000)/0.8 = 887,500 — the buyer's share goes in the denominator, because POTA is the cost at which the buyer's ceiling absorbs everything beyond it. Below POTA: at 900k the ceiling is already crossed (900k > 887.5k), so price = ceiling 950,000, fee = 50,000. At 1,000k: price 950,000, fee = −50,000. At 1,200k: price 950,000, fee = −250,000 — beyond POTA the seller absorbs every rupee of overrun, funding the buyer's discount out of fee, and past a fee of zero the seller loses money outright. This is why sellers stop investing in cost control near POTA, why they price the tail into the target, and why buyers cap liability at the ceiling. Scenario matches: (a) fixed price — scope certainty lets the seller price risk; (b) cost-plus or T&M — unknowns make fixed price a gamble either party regrets; (c) T&M with rate caps — flexible duration, bounded rates.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- (b) as FPIF with heavy contingency: defensible when the buyer has strong estimating data; without it, the ceiling punishes honest sellers.
