# Answer key — CS-98 · Inspection readiness in five weeks

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Triage-then-rehearse readiness

## Reasoning

Gap analysis: model cards for two retrains (remediate — two weeks, generated from pipeline metadata per CS-85's fix); incident log inconsistency (remediate — normalize entries to one template, one week; the inconsistencies are formatting, not substance); hotfix change-control gaps (disclose — the hotfixes shipped under emergency provisions that exist on paper; the disclosure is 'our emergency path worked, our post-hoc documentation lagged; here is the fix' — hiding it invites the inspector to find it and reprice the whole evidence trail). Assembly: weeks 1–2 evidence inventory and card generation; week 3 incident log normalization and the disclosure memo drafted with counsel; week 4 the mock inspection — an internal reviewer with the regulator's public checklist plays inspector and probes the two areas the team is proudest of (pride hides gaps as reliably as fear); week 5 buffer and briefing. Why disclosure beats remediation theater: inspectors are professional gap-finders; a self-disclosed gap with a dated fix reads as control maturity, while a discovered one reads as control failure across everything else — the asymmetry is the whole strategy. The mock inspection in week 4 (not 5) exists because its findings need the buffer week.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Requesting postponement: available, and it converts a five-week sprint into a credibility question — defensible only if the gaps are beyond triage, which these are not.
## Facilitation

Run a five-minute mock inspection on the students' own capstone evidence; the panic is instructive and the gaps are always the same three.
