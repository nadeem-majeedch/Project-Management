## Reasoning

Base: revenue 200k, 350k, 500k, 650k, 800k in months 1–5; net burn 950k, 800k, 650k, 500k, 350k; cumulative 950k, 1.75M, 2.40M, 2.90M, 3.25M — cash 3.2M is exhausted during month 5: the company dies in month 5 with 50k of month-5 spend uncovered. Cut (burn 977.5k): net 777.5k, 627.5k, 477.5k, 327.5k, 177.5k, 27.5k in months 1–6; cumulative 2.3875M at month 5 — cash 812.5k — and month 6 adds 27.5k (cash 785k); month 7 revenue 1.1M exceeds burn: the company turns cash-positive in month 7 with 907.5k remaining. The cut's value: 2.5 extra months and — the nonlinear part — those two months contain the revenue crossover, so the cut doesn't extend death, it converts death into survival. Decision calendar (base plan): month 2 — the raise or the cut is decided (month-5 death with a 3-month decision-to-cash lag means deciding in month 2 is the last responsible moment); month 3 — term sheet or the cut executes. Under the cut: month 4 — the raise conversation resumes from strength (cash-positive trajectory is a different pitch than runway panic); month 6 — growth spending resumes. The calendar's principle: runway arithmetic is only decision-useful when mapped against the decision-to-cash lag.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Cutting 30% instead of 15%: buys more months but cuts into the revenue-driving team (CS-45's capability runway) — the arithmetic must be paired with what the cut amputates.
