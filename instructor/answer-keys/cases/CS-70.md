# Answer key — CS-70 · AI risk-mining pilot

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Evidence-linked mining with ground-truth scoring

## Reasoning

Corpus: one completed project's retrospectives, incident tickets, and change log. Output contract: each mined risk = {statement, causal chain, evidence citation (ticket/retro ID + quote), proposed register fields}; no citation, no entry. Verification: two reviewers (PM + the past project's lead) score each mined risk against the project's actual outcome — was it real, was it caught, would earlier registration have changed a decision? Success criteria: ≥60% of mined risks rated real-and-undocumented, and at least three where earlier registration plausibly changes a decision — that is value, not volume. The scale decision compares reviewer hours per validated risk against hours to produce them manually. The generic-risk failure mode is worse than hallucination: hallucinations are caught by citation checks; plausible generics slip into registers and rot them from inside.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Skipping the pilot and mining live projects: faster, and it violates every verification principle this course teaches; the retrospective corpus is the only place ground truth exists.
## Facilitation

Run the reviewer role yourself on three mined examples; the class scores them blind first, and calibration disagreement is the discussion.
