# Answer key — CS-11 · Tailoring a heavyweight template to a 6-week project

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Control-point tailoring

## Reasoning

Keep what creates or protects a decision: charter (authorization), scope statement (boundaries), schedule (time control), risk register (foresight), change log (scope discipline), closure report (learning). Cut acceptance criteria: most cuts accept 'discovered late' risk owned by the PM; the biggest single risk is cutting the change log, which is how scope creep goes unnoticed. The PMO defense: six documents still cover initiate-plan-control-close; the 34 cuts remove redundancy, not control.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Keeping a one-page combined quality+test plan instead of the closure report: defensible for a 6-week build where quality risk exceeds learning value.
## Facilitation

Ask which cut students would reverse if the project ran 6 months instead of 6 weeks — proportionality is scale-dependent.
