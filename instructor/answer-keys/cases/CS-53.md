# Answer key — CS-53 · Fast-tracking gone wrong

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Dependency-assumption diagnosis

## Reasoning

The fast-track assumed requirements were stable enough to freeze schema against — an assumption nobody tested: the two requirement changes were both foreseeable (registrar had flagged a reporting change in writing two weeks before). Method failure, not luck: the signal existed and was not consulted. The rework loop: schema froze on R7; R9 changed R7's cardinality; rebuild cascaded through three downstream modules. Defensible fast-tracking needs: (1) dependency whose late-side task is genuinely insensitive to early-side variation (soft overlap, not hard coupling); (2) the coupling tested, not assumed — a spike or interface contract; (3) a rework budget named in advance. This case had none of the three. Crash would have cost 2 weeks of money; fast-track cost 5 weeks of rework — 'free' was the most expensive word on the project.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Accepting the two-week slip: with these numbers, clearly correct; the checklist exists so the next team doesn't rediscover this by experience.
