# Answer key — CS-96 · The expert who is the bus factor

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Expertise-reframed transfer

## Reasoning

The knowledge strategy must give the expert a role that transfer serves: make him the architect whose system outlives him — 'the DSL is your design; a design that dies with its designer is a hack, and you don't build hacks' reframes documentation as legacy rather than bureaucracy. Mechanism: not documents — a teaching rotation where he runs fortnightly design sessions on the DSL's decisions (why the scheduler is lazy, why validation is inline) recorded as design rationale; plus a supervised succession (CS-83's standard): the successor executes a full pipeline change from design through deploy with him reviewing, and the succession is complete when the successor's change ships without his edits. The legitimacy read: he is protecting scarcity-value and control — naming him architect converts the scarcity from job security into professional standing, which is the trade that gets cooperation without humiliation. Interim posture: the DSL's two most critical components get a second reviewer on every change this month; no DSL change ships without a second engineer who can explain it; the resignation scenario's exposure is capped by the review rule, not by hope. What the two failed PDFs prove: the format was the problem — documents encode conclusions, sessions and supervised changes transfer reasoning.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Hiring a second DSL expert: solves it with money and time; the design-session mechanism is still needed because a second expert transfers nothing to the team of five.
## Facilitation

Ask what happened to the two PDFs — they died because writing was the goal; in this design, writing is the byproduct of teaching, which is why it survives.
