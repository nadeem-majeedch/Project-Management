# Answer key — CS-86 · The sponsor who wasn't there

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Decision triage with default-with-notice

## Reasoning

Inventory first: of the queued decisions, KYC vendor is the true blocker (contract window closes), pricing is strategic (sponsor's actual job), beta scope is delegable (product lead's call with sponsor visibility). Triage: decisions the sponsor alone can make get a one-page decision memo with a stated default and a by-date; everything else gets decided by the named owner with the sponsor cc'd. Default-with-notice: 'If I hear nothing by Thursday, we proceed with option B' — silence stops being consent and becomes a decision the sponsor watched being made; the mechanism ends the pattern without accusing anyone. Escalation: if two default-with-notice memos expire untouched, the escalation is a calendar request, not an email escalation — 'I need 20 minutes to realign decision rights'; going around is doing the opposite of what the default announced, which the design forbids by construction. The sponsor's disengagement is workload: the one-page memo with a default is the accommodation that makes engagement cheap.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Replacing the sponsor: the org-level fix for a pattern this deep, but premature until default-with-notice has failed twice with visibility.
## Facilitation

Have each student name a decision currently blocked by silence in their own capstone — the default-with-notice mechanism works there on Monday.
