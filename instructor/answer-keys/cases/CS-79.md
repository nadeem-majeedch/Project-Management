# Answer key — CS-79 · Exit clauses for the scoring vendor

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Exit-by-design clauses

## Reasoning

Triggers: for cause — two SLA misses in any rolling three months after cure period (tightened from CS-62's ladder, since the pattern is now documented); for convenience — 90 days' notice with egress fee capped at 6 months' fees; change of control — either party may exit in 60 days (acquisition by a TelcoCare competitor is the real risk). Data egress: full raw + scored history in open format (parquet/CSV with schema contract), 15 business days, cost capped at 400k regardless of volume, completeness verified against row counts TelcoCare holds independently. Transition assistance: named day rates (senior engineer 180k/day) with 20 days guaranteed availability, agreed now — pricing during a crisis is pricing with zero leverage. Anti-lock-in: the vendor maintains a documented API contract and a monthly feature-store export; refusal to maintain portability is itself a for-cause trigger. What the vendor gains: predictability — strong exits priced fairly are cheaper for them than disputed ones, and the commitment often wins the renewal against competitors who promise everything and specify nothing.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Rebuild scoring in-house instead of renewing: the strategic end-state, but the 12–18 month build means the exit clauses are what make the vendor relationship survivable meanwhile.
## Facilitation

Ask who holds the row counts that verify egress completeness — if the answer is the vendor, the clause is decorative.
