# Answer key — CS-85 · Responding to the external AI audit

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Finding-to-control remediation

## Reasoning

Triage: F1 consent-scope drift = critical (regulatory exposure, active) — the two features are masked from the next model build within the week; consent refresh or feature removal decided by governance board, not the product team. F2 missing model cards = high (evidence gap, compounding) — cards for both retrains within two weeks, generated from the training pipeline metadata so they cannot drift again. F3 unreviewed drift alerts = high (silent failure) — alert review enters the weekly operations rhythm with a named owner and an escalation path; three weeks of alert history reviewed now to establish whether drift occurred unobserved. Governance fix per finding: F1 — consent scope becomes a feature-level metadata field checked at pipeline gate (CS-56's gate suite extends); F2 — model card generation is a build artifact, not a document someone owes; F3 — alert review without an owner was monitoring theater; ownership is the control. The pattern: none of the three findings was caused by malice; all three were caused by controls that existed as intentions.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Freezing the product pending remediation: maximal caution, real revenue harm, and it removes the urgency that funds the fix — proportionate response beats maximal response here.
## Facilitation

Ask which finding would have appeared in a student's own capstone audit — all three, is the honest answer; the governance pattern is generic.
