# Answer key — CS-93 · BATNA preparation for the renewal negotiation

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** BATNA-anchored preparation

## Reasoning

TelcoCare's BATNA: the internal replacement — 12–18 months, costed at ~2.5M, viable because CS-79's egress clause guarantees data portability and CS-62's shadow probes de-risk migration; the decision date that makes it real: 'replacement build starts March 1 unless renewal is signed by February 15 at ≤ current+10%.' The vendor's BATNA: losing the account — 3 years of revenue, a reference customer, and the renewal at ANY price is worth more than the 30% premium on a dead account; their weakness is that they priced for a customer with no exit, and the exit clauses weaken that read. Leverage: strong — the documented exit path and the shadow-replacement data; weak — the 12–18 month replacement window means a walk-away costs TelcoCare a year of feature velocity; symmetric — both sides lose real money in a rupture. Walk-away line: current+10% with the CS-79 exit clauses intact (the clauses are worth more than 20 points of price — they are what makes the NEXT negotiation survivable). Opening posture: not grievance-led ('your SLA record') but structure-led: 'Here is our replacement timeline and its trigger date; here is what a renewal needs to contain.' First concession planned: multi-year term for a lower escalator — trading commitment for price is the concession that costs least.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Negotiating without the trigger date: the BATNA becomes a threat instead of an option, and threats without dates get called — the February 15 date is what converts analysis into leverage.
## Facilitation

Ask what the vendor's side of the leverage map says — students who cannot write the counterparty's BATNA have not prepared; they have rehearsed.
