# Answer key — CS-95 · Portfolio rebalance under the mandate

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Constrained portfolio selection

## Reasoning

Discretionary = 5.0 − 2.0 − 1.2 = 1.8M. Feasible sets from {P2 1.5/70, P3 1.0/60, P4 0.8/55, P5 0.7/40}: {P2} = 70 (0.3M unused); {P2,P5} = 110 (2.2M — over); {P3,P4} = 115 (1.8M exactly); {P3,P5} = 100 (1.7M); {P4,P5} = 95 (1.5M); {P2 alone} loses to {P3,P4}. Optimum: P3+P4, score 115. The naive answer — P2, the highest scorer — leaves 0.3M idle and scores 70: 45 points of value abandoned to budget blindness. Governance critique: (1) P5's 40 score may undervalue a 0.7M project that seeds next year's portfolio — scoring models price today's benefits, not options; (2) committed P1 escaped scoring entirely — it was 85 last cycle and might not survive this one, but commitment removed it from scrutiny, which is exactly how zombie projects survive; (3) the mandate consumed 1.2M with no score — governance must force mandates to at least state their regulatory basis and any dead zones they create (P5 cut as the mandate's casualty). What changes the optimum: P4's true cost (if 0.8M hides a 1.0M commitment tail, {P3,P5} at 1.7M wins) — the estimate quality of the marginal projects, held by their PMs, is the information the portfolio review must surface.
