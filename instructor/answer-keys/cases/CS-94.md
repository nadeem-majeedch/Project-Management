# Answer key — CS-94 · Decommissioning the legacy ledger

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Data-kept, system-sunset decommission

## Reasoning

Framework: billing DATA is retained 7 years (regulatory) — exported to the archive with verified checksums and a retrieval SLA of 48h; CODE is retired with a tagged repository snapshot and build instructions; the SYSTEM is sunset — unsupported OS plus a departed maintainer means every month of delay compounds a risk someone else inherits silently. Sunset sequence: month 1 — export and verify archive (the data question is now closed); month 2 — the three departments' quarterly queries are mapped: two move to the new platform's reports, one becomes a scheduled extract owned by the data team (a named replacement per query is what kills 'might need it'); month 3 — read-only notice, cutover memo issued with the date: 'The ledger powers down on the 15th; retrieval requests go to the archive with 48h SLA.' The cutover memo forces the political moment: departments that 'might need it' must name the query by the date — after which the answer is the archive, not the ledger. The real cost of delay: unsupported OS risk is an unowned security exposure; the decommission decision is the risk-transfer decision, and deferring it just re-assigns the risk to whoever notices last.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Virtualizing the ledger and keeping it: defensible for one budget cycle; it moves the cost from visible decommission work to invisible maintenance, and 'might need it' remains undefeated.
## Facilitation

Ask what the archive's retrieval SLA implies the team must build BEFORE sunset — the 48h retrieval is the decommission plan's hidden deliverable.
