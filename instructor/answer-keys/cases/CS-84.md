# Answer key — CS-84 · Weighted scoring and the tie that mattered

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Weighted scoring with sensitivity

## Reasoning

Base: A = 0.3×4 + 0.4×3 + 0.2×5 + 0.1×2 = 3.60; B = 0.3×2 + 0.4×5 + 0.2×3 + 0.1×4 = 3.60 — a tie. Sensitivity (technical 0.45, cost 0.25): A = 1.00+1.35+1.00+0.20 = 3.55; B = 0.50+2.25+0.60+0.40 = 3.75 → B wins. The tie's meaning: the decision is entirely weight-dependent, which is not a scandal — it is information. The honest debrief: 'Under the published weights you scored identically; under the sensitivity weights technical fit dominates and B leads. Your transition score was the lowest in the field; that is the gap to address.' Governance rule: weights are fixed and signed before proposals open, sensitivity weights fixed at the same time; no weight may change after scoring begins — the rule that prevents weight-shopping is temporal, not technical. What the scoring drove: a recommendation, not an appointment — price negotiation and reference checks still run.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Adding a tiebreaker criterion (e.g., local support) post hoc: the classic governance breach; if the criterion matters it belongs in the published matrix.
