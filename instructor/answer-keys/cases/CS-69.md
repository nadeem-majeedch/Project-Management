# Answer key — CS-69 · Communication protocol for a distributed team

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Async-first with decision records

## Reasoning

Channels: chat for coordination (response ≤ 4h work-hours, no decisions); documents/PRs for work products (review ≤ 24h); decision records for anything with consequences (propose → 48h async comment window → owner decides → log in the decision register with context, options, choice, dissent). The weekly sync agenda: decisions already proposed in writing get 10 minutes each — the sync ratifies, it does not originate; anything not pre-read is deferred, ruthlessly. 'Findable three months later' kills chat as a decision store: chat is where decisions go to disappear; the decision register with stable links is the memory. Over-meeting happens because meetings feel like action; documentation feels like bureaucracy until the first dispute is settled by a register link.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Two syncs per week: faster ratification, and it recreates the exclusion the protocol exists to fix; the data engineers' time zone makes it a noon meeting for them.
## Facilitation

Audit the last ten decisions in students' own capstone: how many exist in writing? The gap is usually total.
