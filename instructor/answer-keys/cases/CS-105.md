# Answer key — CS-105 · Capstone feeder: the delivery strategy memo

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Committed strategy with named risks

## Reasoning

Approach: hybrid — predictive spine (18 months, fixed regulatory dates for the data-protection review and the semester-cycle deployment windows) with agile delivery inside (model development and dashboards iterate; the consent framework and privacy review gate them). Governance: three-tier — project board monthly (registrar, faculty senate delegate, data protection officer, sponsor); delivery gates quarterly (data-use approval, model validation per CS-32's DoD, deployment readiness per CS-71's evidence standard); agile teams with working agreements (CS-34) and the model DoD as a quality floor. Risk posture: (1) consent refusal by student body — response: consent design work in month 1, fallback to opt-in cohort analytics; (2) model unfairness across student segments (CS-74's pattern is certain here) — response: slice evaluation in the model DoD, carve-out rules pre-agreed; (3) faculty autonomy resistance — response: faculty senate delegate on the board from day one, engagement before announcement; (4) regulatory scope change — response: the predictive spine's gates make re-planning cheap; (5) benefits attribution dispute — response: CS-104's decomposition method agreed with finance before launch. Team: product owner from the registrar's office (the benefits owner), model lead, platform lead, data governance lead embedded — the DPO relationship is a role, not a meeting. Benefits case: retention delta for flagged students vs matched comparison — owned by the registrar, measured quarterly, reviewed with CS-104's decomposition so deployment and lift shortfalls are separable. The decision-log appendix is the discipline device: every choice above, its alternative, and why it lost.
