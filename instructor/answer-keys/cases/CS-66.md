# Answer key — CS-66 · Two tech leads, one architecture

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Bounded decision with explicit rights

## Reasoning

The stalemate persisted because decision rights were undefined: each lead believed consensus was required, so neither could lose and neither could win. Process: 90-minute decision forum — each lead presents their option against four named criteria (fit to current team skills, operational cost, migration path from the other, failure blast radius); the technical director (not the PM) decides within 48 hours using those criteria; the decision is logged with dissent recorded. Decision rights: implementation choices = owning lead; architecture = technical director on lead recommendation; PM overrides only on cost/schedule grounds, never on technical merit. Reset: 'We spent three weeks where the cost of deciding wrong was smaller than the cost of not deciding; here is how we decide the next one.' The reset names the process failure, not the people.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Split the system: event-driven for the two flows that need it, batch for the rest — often the real answer hiding under the binary framing; legitimate here if the criterion analysis shows different fitness per flow.
## Facilitation

Track what the stalemate cost in tickets blocked — students usually estimate one week; the real number is the lesson.
