# Answer key — CS-102 · Leadership succession during recovery

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Judgment-transfer handover

## Reasoning

Inventory: the documents (recovery plan, decision log, commitment ledger) transfer in week 1; the relationships — sponsor, the two team leads, the vendor's executive sponsor — get joint meetings in weeks 2–3 where the replacement leads and you attend silently (transfer of relationship is by introduction and visible endorsement, never by email); the unwritten context — which commitments are soft, which stakeholder trusts are thin, where the recovery's numbers are honest but fragile — gets written as a private memo to the replacement, not a deck. Deliberate gaps: the two decisions scheduled for month 5 (pricing revision, contractor release) are left unmade — a successor who inherits only executed decisions executes someone else's plan; leaving real decisions is what converts inheritance into ownership. Announcement: to the team — 'the recovery is yours now; the plan works because you made it work; my departure is opportunity, not flight, and I am here for four weeks of whatever you need'; to the sponsor — the continuity facts plus the day you stop having opinions; to the board — the metrics and the successor's name with your endorsement. The difference between transferring plan and judgment: the plan is 40 pages; the judgment is 'when the CPI dips below 0.9 for two weeks, we talk to the sponsor before the numbers do' — that sentence is the handover.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Staying through month 6: maximal continuity, and it converts the successor into a deputy for two more months — four weeks of deliberate overlap beats six of accidental shadowing.
## Facilitation

Ask what happens to a recovery when the departing leader keeps 'helping' after the handover — the deliberate-gaps list exists partly to give the successor room the predecessor's presence would deny.
