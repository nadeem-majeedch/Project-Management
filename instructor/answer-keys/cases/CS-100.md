# Answer key — CS-100 · The secondary-use request

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Consent-scope analysis with alternatives

## Reasoning

Consent analysis: patients consented to treatment coordination — a purpose defined by relationship and expectation; research model-building is a different purpose even when de-identified, because the consent question is about purpose, not identifiability alone. De-identification changes the regulatory exposure, not the ethical gap — and 'partially succeeds' where the data is aggregated beyond re-identification risk, which the request does not claim. Governance path: the board decides in its own cycle — the funding deadline is noted and given zero decisional weight (the script says this out loud: 'the deadline is your constraint, not our criterion'); the board needs the de-identification method, the re-identification risk assessment, and the research protocol before ruling. Alternatives: (1) a consented cohort — prospective consent for the research use, slower and clean; (2) synthetic data generated from the schema and statistics — weaker science, zero consent exposure, available immediately; (3) federated or aggregate statistics that answer the research question without record-level access. The script today: 'The request is outside consent scope as written. The board will consider it in its cycle. Here are three paths that could start this week — synthetic data is the fastest legitimate one.' What makes this expert rather than bureaucratic: the alternatives are real and offered with energy — governance that only says no trains requesters to route around it.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- Broad consent renegotiation with the patient population: the durable fix for the institution, irrelevant to this request's two-week clock.
## Facilitation

Ask what the synthetic-data alternative costs in research validity — the honest answer ('some power, some novelty') is why governance survives: it can be generous without being permissive.
