# Answer key — CS-101 · The four-week crisis: go-live triage

> **INSTRUCTOR ONLY — not published to the site.**

**Model answer:** Ruled war room

## Reasoning

Exit criteria for formal rollback mode: data integrity verified (no further corruption possible), workaround procedures documented and trained for the registrar's staff, daily cost of rollback quantified (3 FTE-equivalents — this number drives everything else). Triage: the 8 blockers are re-verified as blockers (crisis inflates severity), then each gets an owner, a fix estimate, and a verification method; the 14 majors freeze — they enter the backlog and are touched only if re-triaged as blockers; the 60+ minors are formally out of scope until after restart. Classification authority: the tech lead, daily, with the PM overriding only to REMOVE items from the crisis scope (adding is the failure mode). Communication freeze: one status doc, updated twice daily at fixed times, one voice (the PM), audience-mapped (registrar gets operational detail, VC gets risk posture, students get service status) — the five stories die because there is one doc and every inquiry is answered with its link. Go/no-go: decision date day 21 (a week of stabilization margin), criteria: all blockers fixed AND verified under load test, rollback cost projected for semester-start week, and a named fallback (postpone to week 3 of semester with the registrar's manual process — decided now, not under pressure). The date's purpose: crises expand to fill all available time; a fixed go/no-go date converts 'fix everything' into 'be ready to decide on day 21'.

## Alternatives and evaluation criteria

- None recorded.
## Facilitation

Ask which of the 8 blockers might be a major in disguise — the re-verification step catches it, and the answer ('usually two') is why triage starts with doubt.
