## Band→action table (model; students must defend their cut-offs)

| Band | Required response | Funding | Review | Close authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Critical (16–25) | immediate funded response; sponsor visible weekly | contingency + MR if needed | weekly | sponsor |
| High (12–15) | funded action before next gate | contingency line | at each gate | PM |
| Medium (6–11) | watch + trigger defined; action pre-approved but unfunded | on trigger | biweekly | PM |
| Low (≤ 5) | passive accept, log only | none | monthly | PM |

**Defensible boundary logic (the graded content):** 12 is "fund now" because
at P≥3 × I≥4 the expected loss (score) exceeds the typical response cost
(≈1–3% of baseline) — acting is cheaper than watching. 10 and below: response
cost approaches expected loss; pre-approval + trigger dominates. A student
moving High to ≥ 10 must show that arithmetic, not taste.

## Equal-score test (model paragraph)

R1 (12: P3×I4, schedule-dominant) and R5 (12: P4×I3, schedule-dominant via
evidence rework) vs R3 (15: quality-dominant). The two 12s differ in
*mechanism*: R1's action is contractual (vendor SLA — money + legal), R5's is
administrative (evidence templates — calendar + credibility). Same number,
different budget line, different owner, different trigger. The committee sees
"two orange cells"; the table shows one needs the vendor manager and one
needs the audit office.

## Reflection guidance

1. Boundary 12→10: every 10–11 risk gains funded actions — spending moves
   from contingency reserves to the base budget; sponsor funds it, so the
   committee proposing it must own the cost.
2. R3's audited P is defensible at a gate because its *error bar* is
   documented; folklore P hides unknown error — evidence quality outranks
   the score because decisions price the error bar.
3. Levers outside the grid: trigger quality, response speed, owner
   authority — the grid prices the risk, not the management system.
