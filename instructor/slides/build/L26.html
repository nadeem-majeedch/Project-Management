    Negotiation: prepare the floor before the room

    flowchart LR INT["Interests<br>(mine & theirs)"] --> OPT["Options<br>(≥ 3 trades)"] OPT --> BAT["BATNA<br>(best alternative)"] BAT --> RP["Reservation point<br>(walk-away)"]
  • BATNA is power: the side that can walk, negotiates
  • Trade variables, not concessions: scope ↔ schedule ↔ payment ↔ risk buffer
  • CS-36's scope cut: your BATNA is the re-baselined plan — arrive with it costed
    • Speaker notes
    The four-box prep chain is the package's negotiation skeleton. The costed-BATNA point converts negotiation from bluffing to engineering. 5 min.

    Delivering bad news early

  • The fact — one sentence, no adjectives: "CPI is 0.87; EAC breaches funding by 9,600"
  • The cause — verified, not speculated
  • Options — two to three, each costed (crash, de-scope, re-baseline)
  • The ask — the decision you need, by when
  • Ownership — what you will do regardless of their choice
  • Late bad news is expensive bad news; early bad news is a project-management deliverable
    • Speaker notes
    The five-part script is the package's instrument and CS-37's rubric. Connect back to L19: the numbers exist precisely to make this conversation possible. 5 min.

    Discussion

  • When is email the wrong channel for good news — and why does that asymmetry exist?
  • Your BATNA is weak (you cannot walk). What legitimate alternatives to power do you have in the room?
  • Which stakeholder in your capstone gets the most communication — and is that the one who needs it?

    Summary & exit ticket

  • Communication plans serve reader decisions, not sender convenience
  • Negotiation: interests → options → BATNA → reservation point — arrive costed
  • Bad news early = facts, cause, options, ask, ownership
