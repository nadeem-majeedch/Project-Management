L02 · Strategy, Portfolios, Programs & Projects

Four words people use interchangeably — and shouldn't

Week 1 · Unit U1 · CLO1 · 2 hours

Learning objectives

  • Define portfolio, program, project, and operations — and place real work correctly
  • Apply a weighted scoring model to rank candidate projects
  • Select a project bundle under a fixed budget and defend it
  • Explain why strategy alignment outranks raw score
    • Speaker notes
    Anchor with the teaching-package definitions. Quick poll: "predictive or agile — which fits a final-year FYP better?" Flag progressive elaboration as the bridge to L08 tailoring. Timing: ~5 min.

    Four containers, one strategy

    flowchart TD STR["Strategy"] --> PF["Portfolio<br>(all the work we choose to fund)"] PF --> PG["Program<br>(related projects, managed together)"] PG --> P1["Project"] PG --> P2["Project"] PF --> P3["Project<br>(stand-alone)"] P1 --> OPS["Operations<br>(runs the delivered value)"]
  • Portfolio: choosing the work — aligned to strategy
  • Program: coordinating related projects for a combined benefit
  • Project: delivering a unique output
  • Operations: running what was delivered
    • Speaker notes
    Emphasize the different questions each level answers: portfolio = "which?", program = "how do these fit together?", project = "how do we deliver?". 3 min.

    Point out that P1 and P3 tie at 3.3 — a tie is not a bug; it forces a strategy conversation. In CS-03 the tie-break question is marginal value per million rupees. 4 min, board-ready.

    CS / DS in the room

  • CS: a software house juggling a portal rebuild (project), a mobile-app suite (program), and support desk (operations)
  • DS: a retail analytics portfolio — forecasting models, dashboards, MLOps platform build; the ML projects compete for the same data-engineer pool
  • Portfolio management is where the DS talent bottleneck actually gets solved
    • Speaker notes
    The DS example lands the resource-sharing point: two separately 'good' ML projects can be jointly infeasible. Sets up CS-95 rebalancing. 2 min.

    Case anchor:

    CS-03 — Portfolio cut-line: weighted scores are given; the budget forces a cut — and the top-scored project is not in the optimal bundle (verified arithmetic; the trap is the lesson)

    CS-95 — Rebalancing: reweight a live portfolio when strategy shifts mid-year

    Speaker notes
    CS-03 is the in-class worked discussion; the checker verifies the optimal bundle excludes the highest raw score. Say that out loud — it primes the evaluation-criteria discussion.

    Discussion

  • Your university has budget for two of: LMS upgrade, research-data platform, campus app. Which weights would you choose — and would the winners change if strategy shifted to "research excellence"?
  • When does a group of projects deserve program-level coordination rather than independent management?
  • Who should own portfolio decisions: sponsor, PMO, or delivery teams?
    • Speaker notes
    Q1 mirrors CS-03's structure so students practice before the case. Q3 seeds the L28 governance lecture. Take one answer per question; 6–8 min total.

    Summary & exit ticket

  • Portfolio selects, program coordinates, project delivers, operations runs
  • Weighted scoring makes trade-offs explicit — ties included
  • Score is an input to strategy conversation, not a verdict

    • Exit ticket (2 min): from L01's ticket, name one project that should be managed at program level with a sibling project — and say why.

    Speaker notes
    Collect and skim before L03; common wrong answer is any big project. Program requires relatedness and combined benefit, not size.
