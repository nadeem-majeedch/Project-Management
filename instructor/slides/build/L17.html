L17 · Risk Responses, Issues & Reserves

Strategies for threats and opportunities — and who owns each one

Week 9 · Unit U4 · CLO4 · 2 hours

Learning objectives

  • Match response strategies to threats and opportunities — and defend the match
  • Distinguish a risk from an issue (and why the register split matters)
  • Allocate contingency and management reserves to the right owners
  • Write response plans with triggers, owners, and costs

    The response playbook

    ThreatsOpportunities
    Avoid — change the plan so it can't happenExploit — make it certain
    Mitigate — reduce P or IEnhance — raise P or I
    Transfer — move it (insurance, contract)Share — partner for the upside
    Accept — document, watch, no actionAccept — take the free upside
  • Every response has a cost — including acceptance (the watch hours)
  • A response without a trigger is a wish with a deadline
    • Speaker notes
    The trigger point is the package's misconception #2 — 'monitor' is a real strategy with real hours. Ask which strategy a fixed-price contract is (transfer — and CS-24 will complicate that). 5 min.

    Risk vs issue: the register split

    flowchart LR R["Risk<br>(uncertain, scored)"] -->|it happened| I["Issue<br>(certain, owned, dated)"] I --> A["Action log<br>(who, by when)"] A -.->|recurring?| R["Back to risk register"]
  • Issues do not get probabilities — they get owners and dates
  • A risk that fires twice is a process defect wearing a costume — back to the register with a structural response

    The recurring-risk point is the teaching package's live lesson. Connect to L20: issue aging is a control signal. 4 min.

    Summary & exit ticket

  • Four strategies per side; every one costs something — including watching
  • Issues get owners and dates; recurring issues are structural defects
  • Contingency = identified risks, PM-held, spent via change control
