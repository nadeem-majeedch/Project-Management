L14 · Quality & Data-Quality Planning

Quality is planned in, not inspected in — including data quality

Week 7 · Unit U3 · CLO2 · 2 hours

Learning objectives

  • Write quality metrics with thresholds before delivery starts
  • Classify costs of quality (prevention, appraisal, internal/external failure)
  • Define data-quality dimensions and enforce them as executable rules
  • Argue why quality planning is the cheapest quality work you will ever do
    • Speaker notes
    This lecture is the DS cohort's bridge into governance territory (returns at L27). CS-19's eight rules are the practical centerpiece. Timing ~5 min.

    From requirements to quality metrics

    flowchart LR REQ["Requirement<br>(< 5 s lookup)"] --> MET["Metric<br>(p95 latency, 200 users)"] MET --> THR["Threshold<br>(pass/fail line)"] THR --> METHOD["Measurement method<br>(who, when, how)"]
  • A metric without a threshold is a wish; a threshold without a method is a fight waiting to happen
  • Cost of quality: prevention (cheap) → appraisal → internal failure → external failure (a 100× climb, roughly)
    • Speaker notes
    The 4-step chain is from the teaching package. The 100× claim: present as the package's rough industry ratio, not a law. 4 min.

    Data quality: six dimensions, executable rules

    DimensionRule example (customer dataset)
    Completenessphone present in ≥ 98% of active rows
    Validitysignup_date <= today for every row
    Uniquenesscustomer_id duplicated in ≤ 0.1% of rows
    Consistencychurn_date implies status = churned
    Timelinessfeed refreshed within 24 h of source
    Accuracy2% sample re-verified monthly

    (Dimension set follows the classic completeness/validity/uniqueness/consistency/timeliness/accuracy model used in the teaching package)

  • CS-19's exercise: turn eight vague complaints into eight executable rules
  • A rule you cannot run in SQL/validation code is a hope, not a rule
    • Speaker notes
    The complaint→rule conversion is CS-19's core skill and directly feeds the responsible-AI agenda (L27). Ask: which dimension does a duplicate-row bug violate? (uniqueness AND consistency — good argument material.) 6 min.

    CS / DS in the room

  • CS: a registration system where "tested" meant "the demo worked once" — CS-18 fixes metrics with thresholds
  • DS: the churn model trained on a 4%-duplicated dataset — every downstream metric was quietly wrong; the failure was quality planning, not modelling
  • Data quality defects are external failures by the time anyone notices them
    • Speaker notes
    The churn example is the teaching package's DS anchor. Connect: this is why L27 treats data governance as a project-management topic, not an IT chore. 3 min.

    Discussion

  • Who should own a data-quality rule: the data engineer, the analyst, or the data owner from L07?
  • Is 100% data quality ever the right target? What would it cost?
  • Your model's accuracy drops after a source-schema change. Which quality dimension failed — and which process?
    • Speaker notes
    Q1 ties back to L07's RACI/ownership discussion; Q3's expected answer: consistency (technical) with timeliness (process) — the process answer is the graded one. 6 min.

    Summary & exit ticket

  • Metric → threshold → method, agreed before delivery
  • Prevention is the cheapest quality work; external failure is the most expensive lesson
  • Data quality = dimensions turned into executable, owned rules

    • Exit ticket (2 min): write one executable data-quality rule for your capstone's dataset — dimension, threshold, owner.
