L29 · Sustainability & Benefits Realization

The project ends; the value question does not

Week 15 · Unit U6 · CLO6 · 2 hours

Learning objectives

  • Build a benefits map linking outputs → outcomes → strategic benefit
  • Compute the compute footprint of a modelling strategy (energy, CO₂, cost)
  • Trade accuracy against sustainability honestly — with numbers
  • Plan the handover that keeps value flowing after the team dissolves

    The benefits map

    flowchart LR O1["Output:<br>churn-model API"] --> OC1["Outcome:<br>retention calls targeted"] O2["Output:<br>dashboard"] --> OC1 OC1 --> B1["Benefit:<br>churn −15% by FY-end"] OC1 --> B2["Benefit:<br>campaign spend −10%"]
  • Outputs are what you build; outcomes are behavior change; benefits are measured strategic value
  • Every benefit needs: metric, baseline, owner, and a measurement date after the project ends
    • Speaker notes
    The output/outcome distinction is the package's core teaching — students routinely call outputs benefits. The post-project measurement date is what makes it real. 5 min.

    Handover: the closure that prevents reopenings

  • Operations readiness: runbooks, monitoring (L20), support tiers named
  • Knowledge transfer: not a document dump — paired run weeks with the receiving team
  • Benefits owners confirmed in writing before closure (L30's sign-off list)
  • The recurring DS gap: nobody owns model retraining — the drift monitor alerts a team that dissolved (L20's unread alert, again)
    • Speaker notes
    The retraining-ownership gap is the package's signature DS closure failure. It links L14 (rules), L20 (monitors), L23 (gates) into a closure checklist. 4 min.

    CS / DS in the room

  • CS: a shipped portal whose benefits case died with the project team — no owner, no measurement date
  • DS: CS-40's two training strategies: the honest sustainability trade — and the follow-on question (retraining cadence multiplies the footprint)
  • Sustainability is a stated assumption in estimates: energy price, grid factor, retraining frequency

    Discussion

  • Who should own benefits after closure — the sponsor, operations, or the PM? What does each answer create?
  • Is model retraining a project or an operation? What follows for its budget and its governance?
  • Would you ship B (86.9%) when A (87.2%) was promised? Draft the stakeholder message.

    Q2's answer shapes the whole handover design — the ambiguous middle is where DS projects live. Q3 rehearses L26's bad-news craft with sustainability stakes. 6 min.

    Summary & exit ticket

  • Outputs → outcomes → benefits: measured by someone, on a date, after closure
  • Compute footprint is estimable: energy, CO₂, cost per strategy
  • Handover transfers ownership, not documents

    • Exit ticket (2 min): write your capstone's one real benefit — metric, baseline, owner, measurement date.
