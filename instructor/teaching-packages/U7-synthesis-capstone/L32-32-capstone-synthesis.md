# Lecture 32 — Capstone Defense & Course Synthesis

> **Package for:** L32 · Week 16 · Unit U7 · CLO6 · Bloom: Create · **Capstone defense + Final-exam window**
> **Case anchor:** CS-43 (defense dress rehearsal)
> Companion: [`L32` skeleton](../../../docs/lectures/L32-32-capstone-synthesis.md) · [CS-43 brief](../../../docs/cases/CS-43.md) · [defense format](../../../docs/capstone/defense-format.md)

## Learning objectives

1. Run the capstone defense to format: 10-minute presentation + 5-minute rubric-scored Q&A per team.
2. Score defenses against the published rubric with evidence-anchored judgments.
3. Synthesize the course: connect the semester's 20 core ideas back to the six CLOs.
4. Map career and certification pathways (PMP, CAPM, PMI-ACP) onto what this course built.

## Required prior knowledge

- The team's own L27–L31 artifacts (plan v2, red-team log, governance,
  benefits, closure checklist, AI norms).
- The [rubric](../../../docs/capstone/capstone-rubric.md) and
  [defense format](../../../docs/capstone/defense-format.md) — both published
  since week 12.

## Teaching notes

Defense day has two audiences: the teams (defend the *plan*, not the slides)
and the course (synthesize two semesters' worth of judgment into something
students carry out). Run the mechanics tight — the format page is the law:
10 + 5 per team, rubric-scored, peer scores collected separately as feedback
only.

The Q&A discipline (from the format page): questions probe **judgment** —
"SPI 0.85 at month 3, what do you do?", "why accept rather than transfer?",
"which estimate would you re-derive first?" — and "the template said so" is
a failing defense. As examiner, hold the follow-up discipline: one probe per
claim, then move; the goal is evidence-anchored scoring, not gotchas.

The synthesis segment is *earned*, not recited: teams map the 20 core ideas
(see worked example) to where they personally used them this semester —
defenses earlier in the session supply the evidence. Certification framing
stays honest: this course's vocabulary maps to PMP/CAPM exam domains, but
*experience + judgment* are what the certification community actually
certifies around; the course is the scaffold, not the credential.

## Definitions & concepts

- **Defense rubric dimensions** — completeness, internal consistency,
  tailoring justification, risk realism, communication, team process
  evidence, individual contribution (weights on the rubric page).
- **Evidence-anchored scoring** — every rubric level cites a plan location or
  Q&A moment; unanchored scores are feedback noise.
- **The 20 core ideas (course synthesis)** — the iron triangle's limits ·
  life-cycle choice by uncertainty · charter as authorization contract ·
  stakeholder movement plans · WBS 100% rule · estimates as distributions ·
  CPM/float as risk geometry · levelling as a recorded trade · reserves as
  two different kinds of money · quality metrics as decisions · DQ gates ·
  normalized risks with owners/triggers · P×I thresholds · EMV & simulation
  honesty (P80) · response completeness (owner/trigger/residual) ·
  baselines under change control · EVM's readings and limits · flow metrics
  (Little's Law) · model gates & fairness bounds · governance and the
  integrity ladder.
- **Certification map** — CAPM (vocabulary entry), PMP (experience-based),
  PMI-ACP (adaptive practice); this course ≈ CAPM-level vocabulary + applied
  judgment beyond it.

## Practical examples

**CS defense moment:** a team's Gantt shows testing in parallel with
development; the examiner asks "what does your backward pass say about the
test task's float?" — the team that *re-derives* live on the whiteboard
scores the internal-consistency dimension; the team that says "the tool
showed it" does not. That contrast is the course in one minute.

**DS defense moment:** the fairness threshold question — "your gate says
≤ 5 points; who set that, and what happens at 5.2?" The mature answer cites
the L23/L28 governance chain (who decided, evidence, the freeze action from
the L20 dashboard); the immature one renegotiates the threshold live. Same
numbers, opposite scores.

## Worked example

**Rubric scoring walkthrough (board, one fictional team):**

| Dimension (weight) | Evidence cited | Level (0–3) | Why not higher |
|---|---|---|---|
| Completeness (20%) | all baselines + subsidiary plans; DQ rules present | 3 | — |
| Internal consistency (20%) | stress tests logged; one seam (budget↔risk) repaired with written trail | 2 | contingency re-derivation not simulated (L16 evidence absent) |
| Tailoring (15%) | 7 cuts each with decision served | 3 | — |
| Risk realism (15%) | owners + triggers present; EMV on one risk; P80 derived | 2 | opportunities absent |
| Communication (15%) | one-page sponsor dashboard; timeboxed defense | 2 | benefits map not read in the 10 min |
| Process evidence (10%) | 3 sprints, WIP limits, retro notes | 3 | — |
| Individual contribution (5%) | memos consistent; one distinct lead | 2 | two memos near-identical (flagged) |

Weighted: (3×20 + 2×20 + 3×15 + 2×15 + 2×15 + 3×10 + 2×5) = 60+40+45+30+30+30+10
= 245 of 300 → **≈ 82%** band → A− territory per the grading scale *before*
the instructor-decision conversion (grading page warning box). The walkthrough
models the evidence-anchor discipline examiners must keep under time
pressure.

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| "The defense is a demo." | It is a *plan* defense: baselines, trade-offs, governance — demos of artifacts serve the argument, never replace it. |
| "Q&A is gotcha theater." | Probes are the *rubric's instrument* for judgment; a team that answers with re-derivation and named trade-offs is doing the course's job out loud. |
| "Higher scope = better grade." | The rubric rewards consistency, tailoring, and realism — a smaller, traceably reasoned plan outscores an inflated one. |
| "Finishing the course = being a PM." | The credential ladder needs experience years; the course supplies vocabulary + judgment scaffolding — the map is on the textbooks page. |
| "Peer scores count toward the grade." | Peer scoring is structured feedback only (rubric page); mixing it into grades corrupts both signals. |

## Classroom activities

1. **Defenses (100 min):** per the format page; instructor scores with
   evidence anchors; peers score separately; timekeeper enforces 10+5.
2. **Synthesis mapping (during transitions):** 20-ideas wall — teams pin
   where each idea "bit" them this semester (one line per pin).
3. **Course close (15 min):** certification pathways honest map; final-exam
   logistics (window, format, AI rules per policy); the L30 retro's committed
   change, verified publicly.

## Discussion questions

*(These run *between* defenses as open floor, addressed to the class:)*

1. Across today's defenses, which rubric dimension separated teams most — and what early-semester artifact would have predicted it?
2. Which of the 20 ideas did you *fight* hardest — and what changed your mind?
3. What is the one question you hope no examiner asks your team — and what would an honest answer sound like?

## Practical exercise

**In session (CS-43, if scheduled as dress rehearsal before defense day):**
full dress rehearsal — 10+5 with rubric scoring by peers; fixes applied to
the defense pack. **On defense day:** the defense itself is the exercise;
individual contribution memos due with the pack (LMS, private).

## Formative assessment (exit ticket — course close)

1. Name the four defense rubric dimensions you'd call hardest to score well — and what evidence each wants.
2. Your single best-learned instrument this semester — and where it appears in the 20-ideas wall.
3. One certification pathway you'll investigate within a year, and why.

  missing pack = the format page's gate.

  [defense-format page](../../../docs/capstone/defense-format.md)) and your
  scoring sheet with evidence-anchor columns.

  16 — defense day is not the rehearsal.
