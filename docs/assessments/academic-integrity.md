# Academic Integrity & Responsible AI

University policy applies in full; this page states the course-specific
interpretation. When in doubt, ask **before** submitting.

## Integrity commitments

1. **Individual work is individual writing.** Discussing cases with peers is
   expected and healthy; submitting prose or artifacts another person wrote
   (or a template that erases your judgment) is not.
2. **Cite what you use.** Standards by edition/year; web sources with URL and
   access date; any framework, chart, or dataset beyond the course's own.
3. **Do not fabricate.** Invented data, invented stakeholder quotes, invented
   "simulation results," or citations you did not read are integrity
   violations — in coursework and in professional practice alike.
4. **Team work means visible contribution.** The capstone's individual
   accountability memo is a graded integrity artifact.

## Responsible use of AI tools

AI assistants (chatbots, code generators, summarizers) are **permitted** in
this course under the following contract:

| # | Rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Disclose** | One line in every submission: which tool, for what (drafting, exploration, critique). A disclosure template is below. |
| 2 | **You verify** | You own every fact, number, and citation you submit. AI-generated content is a draft to check, never a source. |
| 3 | **No fabrication laundering** | Using AI to invent data, references, or "simulation output" is fabrication — the violation is not the tool, it is the invented content. |
| 4 | **Exams and quizzes are AI-free** | In-person assessments: no devices unless the instructor announces otherwise. |
| 5 | **Case portfolio voice** | Portfolio analyses must read like your judgment. Templated AI prose across 12 analyses is trivially detectable and scores 0. |

### Disclosure template

```text
AI disclosure: [tool name + version] was used for [drafting the risk
register structure / exploring estimate ranges / critiquing my first
draft]. All facts, calculations, and final wording are mine; sources:
[...].
```

### Where AI genuinely helps in this course

- Stress-testing a risk list for blind spots (then verifying each risk is
  real — see CS-42, which makes exactly this exercise).
- Rehearsing defense questions before the capstone.
- Drafting structure for a case analysis you then fill with your judgment.

### Where it fails you

- Computing CPM floats or EVM sets (it will produce plausible wrong numbers
  — show your own pass).
- Any invented citation: AI tools do generate non-existent references;
  submitting one is a violation regardless of intent.

## Violations & process

- First violation: 0 on the component + instructor conversation.
- Fabrication of data/citations or repeat violation: escalation per
  university policy.
- The case portfolio is the integrity honeypot by design: twelve individual
  analyses across different domains make template-sharing statistically
  obvious.
