# Assessment Strategy

## Design principles

1. **Constructive alignment.** Every CLO is taught (CLO×lecture matrix),
   practiced (cases/labs), and assessed (formal components). The chain is
   published — nothing on the grade rides on hidden criteria.
2. **Judgment over recall.** Memory checks (quizzes, exam sections) exist but
   carry 15% combined. The rest of the grade is earned by producing and
   defending artifacts: plans, registers, networks, dashboards, analyses.
3. **Individual + team evidence.** Assignments and exams are individual; the
   capstone is a team deliverable with an individual accountability memo —
   both skill sets are graded.
4. **Feedback cadence.** Something is graded and returned every ~2 weeks;
   nothing except the final exam lands after week 16 has begun.

## The eight components and why each exists

| ID | Component | Weight | Exists to prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Quizzes (best 2 of 3) | 10% | Baseline vocabulary and concept recall (CLO1) |
| A2 | Assignment 1 — Scope & Schedule | 10% | CLO2 artifact production: WBS, PERT, CPM, Gantt |
| A3 | Assignment 2 — Risk Register | 10% | CLO4 identification → response discipline |
| A4 | Assignment 3 — EVM Analysis | 10% | CLO3/CLO4 quantitative diagnosis |
| A5 | Midterm (L01–L20) | 20% | Individual quantitative gate: CPM, PERT, EVM under exam conditions |
| A6 | Case Portfolio (12 × 300–500 words) | 10% | Breadth of judgment across domains; individual writing voice |
| A7 | Capstone Plan + Defense | 25% | CLO5/CLO6 integration, tailoring, and defense under questioning |
| A8 | Final Exam | 5% | Comprehensive sweep, emphasis L21–L32 (CLO1, CLO6) |

## CLO evidence map

!!! note "CLO3 formal coverage"
    CLO3 is deliberately assessed as one strong formal gate (the midterm's
    cost/EVM/quality problem set) plus continuous case practice. This is a
    recorded design decision — revisit after first delivery (see planning/05 §2).

## Workload distribution across the semester

| Weeks | Assessment load | Typical individual hours/week |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | none yet + case portfolio opens | 3–4 |
| 3–4 | Lab 1–2, Quiz 1 (L04) | 4–5 |
| 5–7 | Labs 3–5, Quiz 2 (L12), A2 released L14 | 6–7 |
| 8–10 | Labs 7–8, A2 due L16, A3 L19, A4 released L19, **midterm window** | 8–10 |
| 11–13 | A4 due L21, Quiz 3 (L26), Labs 9–11 | 6–7 |
| 14–15 | capstone plan draft (L27), Labs 12 | 8–10 |
| 16 | capstone defense (L32), final exam | 6–8 |

Peak load (weeks 8–10) coincides with the midterm — plan other courses
accordingly.

## How rubrics keep grading honest

Each rubric dimension is scored 0–3 with published descriptors
([capstone rubric](../capstone/capstone-rubric.md) as the model). Unspecified
numeric conversions (e.g., rubric level → percentage) are **instructor
decisions** — flagged as such on the [grading page](grading.md) rather than
invented here.
