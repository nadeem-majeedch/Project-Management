# Course Policies & Student Expectations

Assessment-specific rules (late penalties, re-grades, missed quizzes) live in
[Assessment policies](../assessments/policies.md); integrity and AI use in
[Academic integrity](../assessments/academic-integrity.md). This page covers
everything else.

## Attendance & participation

- Lectures are **in-person and case-driven**: most sessions include a graded
  in-class activity built on the session's anchor case. There is no separate
  attendance grade, but case work cannot be replicated outside class.
- Arrive on time; case work starts within the first 15 minutes.
- If you must miss: check a peer's notes + this site; the dropped-lowest
  quiz policy (best 2 of 3) absorbs occasional conflicts.

## Communication

| Channel | Use |
|---|---|
| LMS announcements | Official course business — check before every lecture |
| Unit discussion thread (LMS) | Content questions, case clarifications — public so everyone benefits |
| Instructor email | Personal matters only; expect one-business-day reply |
| Office hours | Per posted schedule; capstone teams please bring an agenda |

## Workload expectations

This is a **3 + 1 credit-hour** course: 64 contact hours plus roughly
6–8 h/week of individual work (cases, labs, assignments, portfolio).
Assignment weeks (A2–A4) and the capstone sprint (weeks 14–16) peak higher —
the [assessment strategy](../assessments/assessment-strategy.md) shows the
distribution so you can plan.

## Devices & materials

- Laptops are needed for labs (Gantt tool, Python notebook for the Monte
  Carlo lab) — a shared machine is acceptable.
- Phones are fine for in-class polling; not for case work.

## Recorded lectures & materials

Lecture slides are posted after each session on this site; sessions are not
recorded by default. Case discussions are the irreplaceable part — the
slides alone are not the course.

## Audit / visiting students

Auditors are welcome to attend and join case teams informally, but cannot
submit graded work or join the capstone defense without enrolling.

## Student expectations (what good looks like)

1. **Do the pre-work:** read the anchor case brief before the lecture.
2. **Bring your voice:** case work is graded on judgment and defense, not
   agreement with the instructor.
3. **Own your estimates:** "the template said so" is never an acceptable
   defense — in this course or in your career.
4. **Disclose AI use** per the [integrity policy](../assessments/academic-integrity.md).
5. **Lift your team:** capstone is a team grade with an individual memo;
   free-riding is a documented, graded offense.
