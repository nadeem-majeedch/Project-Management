# Lab 16 — Capstone Integration Pack

## 1. Objectives
1. Assemble your project's planning artifacts into the capstone pack structure.
2. Run the cross-artifact consistency audit (numbers agree across charter, WBS, schedule, budget, risk, quality).
3. Identify the three weakest artifacts and fix them before the plan draft deadline (L27).

## 2. Background & scenario
The capstone ([charter & scope](../capstone/capstone-charter.md), due as a
draft at L27, defended at L32) is not a new project — it is your *own*
approved project planned with everything this course taught. This lab is the
assembly checkpoint: teams bring every artifact produced since Lab 1, run the
consistency audit, and leave with a punch list. The draft that follows one
week later grades the *pack*, not the pieces.

## 3. Required tools
- All your prior lab outputs (Labs 1–15 artifacts, tailored to your own project)
- [Capstone charter requirements](../capstone/capstone-charter.md) + [capstone rubric](../capstone/capstone-rubric.md)
- Templates as needed (revisit [templates index](../templates/index.md))

## 4. Step-by-step instructions
1. **Structure check (20 min).** Map your artifacts against the capstone pack table of contents (charter, scope, WBS + dictionary, schedule + network, cost baseline + S-curve, register + P×I, quality plan, comms plan, monitoring plan, life-cycle + tailoring justification). Missing sections go on the punch list.
2. **Cross-consistency audit (40 min).** The checks that catch integration errors:
   - WBS hours = budget labor hours; S-curve cumulative = cost baseline exactly.
   - Dictionary acceptance criteria = quality plan verification rows.
   - Register High/Critical = funded actions; triggers = monitoring dashboard rows.
   - Network finish = Gantt finish = charter milestone dates; deadline constraint honored.
   - Stakeholder register IDs = communication plan rows = P×I "committee view" audience.
   Record PASS/FAIL per check; fix or flag.
3. **Tailoring statement (20 min).** For your life cycle: which artifacts did you *cut or merge*, and why (L08 criteria: volatility, regulatory exposure, feedback cadence, distribution)? The rubric rewards justified tailoring, not template completeness.
4. **Weakest-three fix sprint (30 min).** Team picks the three weakest artifacts by the rubric's dimensions; 30 minutes of focused repair; punch list for the rest.
5. **Peer swap (15 min).** Swap packs with another team; each marks the two most confusing sections (this is exactly what the defense panel will probe).
6. **Refine (out of lab, 2 h).** Apply the punch list; draft plan due L27.

## 5. Your tasks (checklist)
- [ ] Structure map against capstone ToC complete
- [ ] All six cross-consistency checks run and recorded
- [ ] Tailoring statement with L08 criteria
- [ ] Weakest three repaired or scheduled
- [ ] Peer feedback captured and triaged

## 6. Expected outputs
`capstone-pack-draft/` (assembled artifacts) + `consistency-audit.md` +
punch list. Submit via LMS; the graded draft follows at L27.

## 7. Reflection questions
1. Which cross-consistency check failed for your team, and what root cause does that failure share with real-world project reporting failures?
2. What did your tailoring cut — and what will the defense panel attack about that cut?
3. The rubric grades integration, not artifacts. Name one way your pack is *more* than the sum of its files.

## 8. Assessment rubric (pass / refine)
| Dimension | Pass | Refine |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | all ToC sections present or justified-cut | gaps silent |
| Consistency | all checks run, failures fixed/flagged | assumed |
| Tailoring | criteria-based justification | "we kept everything" |
| Repair | weakest three improved | cosmetic edits |
| Peer feedback | captured + triaged | ignored |
